Summary

1. Cleanliness has declined steadily through the window

The cleanliness factor fell from 0.931 to 0.847 over the reporting window, a 7.9% shortfall against the HEI design value of 0.920. The decline is monotonic rather than event-driven, which is consistent with progressive waterside fouling rather than a single upset. Terminal temperature difference tracked the same slope, rising from 2.6 °F to 3.9 °F

2. Back pressure is lifting the unit heat rate

Shell back pressure averaged 2.71 inHgA against a 2.50 inHgA design point, peaking at 2.91 inHgA during the 22–26 August high-ambient period. The derived heat-rate penalty averaged 1.05% and reached 1.42% at the end of the window · roughly 4,800 MMBTU of additional fuel across the month at the observed load factor

3. Air in-leakage is elevated but stable

In-leakage held between 2.2 and 2.6 SCFM against a 1.0 SCFM design allowance. The stability of the signal points to a fixed leak path rather than a developing one; it contributes to, but does not dominate, the observed vacuum loss

Recommendations

1. Schedule a waterside tube cleaning at the next available outage window

2. Perform a helium leak survey on the LP turbine gland and vacuum breaker penetrations

3. Verify cooling-water flow against the design 156,000 gpm; the observed 152,300 gpm suggests partial macrofouling at the inlet screens
